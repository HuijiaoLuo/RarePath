# The 10× case: comparable natural-history data in months, not years

> **Status: hypothesis.** The timelines below come from public study records plus stated assumptions. They have not been measured with a real patient group. The section "Assumptions to validate next" lists what has to be true, and how to test it.

## The milestone

A patient group for **GM1 gangliosidosis** (no approved treatment) needs **natural-history data**: how the disease progresses without treatment, measured in a consistent way. Drug developers and regulators need this before a trial can show a treatment works. The FDA notes that natural-history data "may provide an untreated, external control group for use as the comparator" and recommends starting such studies early, "even before an investigational therapeutic drug has been identified" ([FDA, 2019](https://www.fda.gov/media/122425/download)).

The milestone we compare: **the date the group has comparable natural-history data it can use with partners.**

## Today's route: build a new study

| Step | Typical time | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Realise a new study is needed and find clinical partners | months | Assumption, to be checked with patient groups |
| Design, ethics approval, site set-up | months | Assumption |
| Follow children prospectively | **about 3.5 years** | PRONTO (NCT05109793), a prospective GM1/GM2 study, ran from 22 Feb 2022 to 16 Sep 2025 (ClinicalTrials.gov) |
| Data analysed and shared | **often years more** | RETRIEVE (NCT04470713) collected data from Jul 2019 to Oct 2021; the paper appeared in Dec 2024 ([PubMed 39639297](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39639297)) |

The FDA also notes that prospective studies "will generally require more time … than the collection of existing data" ([FDA, 2019](https://www.fda.gov/media/122425/download)).

**Time to the milestone: roughly 4 years or more.**

## Why this could be about 10×

About 4 years against about 4 months, if the assumptions below hold. The gain does not come from doing a study faster. It comes from **not starting a new study when a suitable one already exists**. RarePath shows on the first screen that four studies already include GM1 together with GM2, and that two of the natural-history studies (PRONTO and RETRIEVE) have finished collecting data.

## Assumptions to validate next

1. **Data access is possible within months.** Ask Azafaros (PRONTO sponsor) and NTSAD whether patient groups can access summary or patient-level data, and under what agreement.
2. **The existing study fits.** PRONTO covers late-infantile and juvenile onset only (its title says so), so it does not help families with infantile GM1. RarePath links to the live record, but an expert must confirm the fit.
3. **The diseases can be compared, not pooled.** The RETRIEVE authors concluded GM1, GM2 and Gaucher type 2 "should not be pooled" in trials because survival differs ([PubMed 39639297](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39639297)). RarePath therefore suggests comparing outcome measures and study design, not merging patients. It already labels Gaucher type 2 "needs review".
4. **Discovery is the bottleneck we think it is.** Interview 3–5 patient-group leaders: how long did it take them to find related studies and partners?

## Where the 10× does not hold

If no existing study or registry fits, RarePath only saves discovery time, and the group still has to build a study. RarePath then says so plainly and lists what to look for next. That honest gap is part of the product.
